# Field guide

Vocabulary for describing what you see and hear, plus rough benchmarks. The benchmarks are rules of thumb from common practice, not laws. Use them to say whether a number is fast, slow or typical for the format, and say "(rough benchmark)" when you lean on one.

## Hooks

The hook is the first 1–3 seconds (Shorts, Reels, TikTok) or first 5–30 seconds (long-form). Name the type, then name each channel it uses: picture, spoken words, on-screen text, sound.

| Hook type | What it does | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Result first | Shows the end state before the process | "This is what $500 of groceries looks like after…" |
| Bold claim / contrarian | Says something that contradicts belief | "Stop drinking water in the morning." |
| Question | Opens a gap the viewer wants closed | "Why do planes never fly over Tibet?" |
| Curiosity gap / withheld object | Shows or names a thing without explaining it | Close-up of an odd object, "Nobody knows what this is for" |
| Pattern interrupt | Something visually or sonically unexpected | Smash cut, loud noise, upside-down shot |
| In medias res | Starts in the middle of action | Mid-sentence, mid-fall, mid-argument |
| Direct callout | Names the viewer | "If you're over 30 and still renting…" |
| Stakes / countdown | Sets a cost or clock | "I have 24 hours to…" |
| Listicle promise | Promises a count | "5 apps that feel illegal to know" |
| Story open | "So this happened…", a cold open on emotion | |
| Demonstration | The visual itself is the hook (satisfying, skill, transformation) | |
| Social proof | Numbers, credentials, famous names | "I asked 100 millionaires…" |

Useful hook measurements from facts.md: time of the first word, words in the first 3 s, cuts and on-screen changes in the first 3 s, audio level of the first 3 s vs the rest, whether text is on screen in frame 1.

**Promise moment**: the time when the viewer learns what they'll get by staying. In strong short-form it falls in the first 2 s; in long-form, the first 15–30 s.

## Structure and beats

Common skeletons:
- **Hook → Setup → Value beats → Payoff → CTA** (explainers, listicles)
- **Hook → Problem → Agitate → Solution → Proof → CTA** (ads, UGC)
- **Cold open → Title → Act 1/2/3 → Resolution** (story, documentary)
- **Challenge**: stakes → attempt → setback → twist → result
- **Countdown list**: #5 … #1, best saved for last, with a teased "#1 will surprise you"
- **Before → Process → After** (transformations, tutorials, makeovers)
- **Loop**: the last line or frame flows back into the first, so the replay feels seamless

Beat vocabulary: setup, inciting moment, turn, reveal, payoff, button (a small final laugh or line after the payoff), stinger, tag, CTA, end screen.

Label each beat with start–end times, its job, and the device it uses.

## Retention devices

- **Open loop**: a question raised early and answered late ("…but the third one is what actually worked").
- **Re-hook**: a fresh mini-hook mid-video, often near the 25%, 50% and 75% marks or right before most-replayed peaks.
- **Pattern interrupt**: a shift in shot, sound, pace or tone every few seconds.
- **Progress signal**: numbers ("#3"), progress bars, chapter cards, checklists.
- **Visual payoff**: matching what's said with what's shown at the same moment.
- **Escalation**: each beat bigger than the last.
- **Callback**: a reference to something earlier.
- **Tease of the next video / part 2**.
- **Comment bait**: a deliberate small mistake, a question, a controversial ranking.
- **Loop ending**: the final words complete the first sentence.

## Shots and framing

Shot types: talking head (A-roll), b-roll, screen recording, motion graphic, kinetic text, stock footage, meme/reaction clip, UGC/selfie, product shot, POV, over-the-shoulder, top-down (flat lay), green screen (creator in front of an image), split screen, picture-in-picture, duet/stitch.

Framing: extreme close-up (ECU), close-up (CU), medium close-up (MCU, chest up), medium (MS, waist up), wide (WS), extreme wide (EWS). Note headroom, whether the eyes are on the upper third, and where the caption-safe area is.

Angle: eye level, high angle, low angle, Dutch (tilted), overhead.

Camera: locked-off (tripod), handheld, gimbal, phone selfie, webcam, multi-cam (two angles of a talking head).

Look: colour grade (warm, teal-orange, desaturated, high-contrast, pastel, flat/log), key light (soft, hard, ring light reflection in the eyes), practicals in frame, set dressing, wardrobe colours.

## Transitions and motion

- **Hard cut**: instant.
- **Jump cut**: same framing, time skipped; removes pauses in talking heads.
- **Punch-in / zoom cut**: same shot cropped tighter on a cut, often on emphasised words. In the facts this often appears as a hard cut between near-identical frames, or as an uncertain change.
- **Smash cut**: an abrupt cut to a contrasting scene or sound.
- **Match cut**: shape, motion or action continues across the cut.
- **J-cut / L-cut**: audio of the next shot starts early / audio of the previous shot runs late.
- **Dissolve / cross-fade, fade to black, dip to white**.
- **Whip pan, spin, glitch, zoom blur, light leak**: stylised transitions, often with a whoosh.
- **Speed ramp**: slowing or speeding within a shot.
- **Ken Burns**: slow pan or zoom on a still.
- **Keyframed zoom**: slow push-in during a talking head to add energy.

Pace words: frantic (< 1 s per shot), fast (1–2 s), brisk (2–4 s), measured (4–8 s), slow (> 8 s).

## On-screen text and captions

Describe:
- **Role**: captions (the words being said), headline/title card, label, list number, callout, lower third (name/title), meme text, CTA.
- **Font**: family style (geometric sans, condensed sans, rounded, serif, handwritten, monospace) and the closest free font. Common matches: Montserrat (ExtraBold/Black), Poppins, Inter, Roboto, Bebas Neue, Anton, Oswald, The Bold Font (closest free: Anton or Montserrat Black), Komika Axis, Proxima Nova (free: Montserrat), Helvetica/Arial (free: Inter), TikTok Sans/Classic, Instagram's Classic (free: Roboto or Inter).
- **Weight and case**: ALL CAPS, Title Case, sentence case.
- **Colour, stroke and shadow**: e.g. white fill, 4–6 px black stroke, soft drop shadow; highlight colour for key words (yellow `#FFE600`, green `#3CFF00`, red, brand colour).
- **Size**: as a share of frame height (captions on vertical video are often 5–8% of the height per line).
- **Position**: lower third, centre, upper third; kept inside the safe area (on 9:16, avoid the bottom ~20% and the right ~15% where the platform UI sits).
- **Chunking**: words per caption (1, 2–3, a full line), lines per caption.
- **Animation**: pop/scale-in per word, karaoke highlight, typewriter, slide-up, bounce, shake on emphasis.
- **Emoji and icons** next to key words.

On animation with a shared dark background (space, night), a hard cut between scenes often changes only part of the frame and shows up as a step change. Glance at those tiles before trusting the pace.

Captions that track speech produce many "step changes" in facts.md (a new caption every 0.3–0.8 s). Text that changes less often than speech suggests headline-style text.

## Voice delivery

- **Pace**: words per minute across the talking span, and while actually talking (articulation rate).
- **Pause use**: pauses before reveals ("the answer is… [pause] nothing"), after questions, jump-cut-removed pauses (very few pauses ≥ 0.35 s plus many cuts = pauses edited out).
- **Emphasis**: which words get louder, higher or stretched; typically numbers, contrast words ("never", "actually", "only"), and the payoff noun.
- **Pitch**: monotone (< ~4 semitones p10–p90 spread), conversational (~4–8), animated (> ~8). Measured on voice; music or effects under the voice can inflate it.
- **Sentence endings**: falling (assertive, authoritative), rising (questioning, upbeat, "uptalk"), flat (deadpan, measured). A high share of falling endings with short sentences reads as confident and punchy.
- **Sentence length**: short (< 8 words) is punchy and suits Shorts; long (> 15) is narrative.
- **Archetype**: describe it, e.g. "high-energy hype narrator", "calm documentary voice", "deadpan comedian", "friendly teacher", "whisper ASMR", "breathless storyteller". Add age range, warmth, energy, accent family, mic distance (close and intimate vs roomy). Never name a real person to copy.
- **Point of view**: first person ("I tried…"), second person ("you need…"), third-person narration.

## Sound

- **Music bed**: present or not, mood (tense, upbeat, lo-fi, cinematic, comedic, trap, phonk, piano), level under the voice. Talking content commonly sits the music 15–25 dB under the voice; music-led content much closer.
- **Music edits**: drops or stops on reveals, beat-synced cuts, a switch of track at a new section, a riser into the payoff.
- **Sound effects**: whoosh on transitions, pop/click on text, ding on a correct point, record scratch, bass hit/boom on reveals, camera shutter, typing, cash register, ambient room tone.
- **Silence**: a hard music stop before a punchline is a strong device.
- **Loudness**: integrated LUFS; platforms normalise loudness, so very loud masters gain nothing on most of them, but short-form audio still tends to be mastered loud.

## Packaging

Title (length, structure, numbers, brackets, emotional words, curiosity gap), thumbnail or cover (face and expression, text of 0–4 words, contrast, arrows or circles, colours), first frame for Shorts/Reels (which is often the cover), description, hashtags, pinned comment, on-screen title in the first second.

## Audience signals

- **Most replayed** (YouTube heatmap): what's on screen and said at each peak. Peaks often sit on reveals, demonstrations, jokes, fast information worth rewinding, or controversial moments. A peak at the very start is normal and not interesting.
- **Top comments**: what people quote (the memorable line), argue about (the comment bait), ask for (the next video), or praise (the strongest device).
- **Ratios** (rough benchmarks, vary a lot by niche and platform):
  - Likes per 1,000 views: ~20–40 typical on YouTube long-form; ~40–80+ strong. Short-form often 30–100.
  - Comments per 1,000 views: ~1–3 typical; 5+ means the video provokes discussion.
  - Views per follower: ~0.1–0.3 is typical for an established YouTube channel; > 1 means the video travelled far beyond the creator's audience (a breakout). On TikTok and Reels distribution is less tied to followers, so this ratio swings much more.
  - Views per day only compare fairly between videos of similar age.

## Benchmarks

Rough, for describing numbers, not for judging:

| Measure | Slow | Typical | Fast |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speaking pace (wpm, talking span) | < 130 | 140–170 | > 180 (hype Shorts often 190–230) |
| Average shot length, Shorts/Reels/TikTok | > 4 s | 1.5–3 s | < 1.2 s |
| Average shot length, YouTube talking-head explainer | > 8 s | 3–6 s | < 2.5 s |
| Average shot length, documentary / video essay | > 10 s | 4–8 s | < 3 s |
| Cuts per minute, short-form | < 15 | 20–40 | > 45 |
| Visual change (cut or on-screen change) | every > 5 s | every 1.5–3 s | every < 1 s |
| First word | > 1.5 s | 0.2–1 s | at 0 s |
| Pauses ≥ 0.35 s per minute | > 15 (unedited) | 5–12 | < 4 (pauses cut out) |
| Pitch spread p10–p90 | < 4 st (monotone) | 4–8 st | > 8 st (animated) |
| Integrated loudness | < -20 LUFS (quiet) | -16 to -12 LUFS | > -10 LUFS (very loud) |
| Bed under voice | > 30 dB (none) | 15–25 dB | < 12 dB (music-forward) |
